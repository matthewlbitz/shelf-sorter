Sorting CDs with Shelf Sorter

Shelf Sorter keeps track of where every CD needs to go while you physically move the CDs through the station.

The most important rule: Never reorder or flip a stack unless the instructions explicitly tell you to. The sorter remembers the physical order of every working stack.

STEP 1

Choose an Unsorted Shelf

Start by choosing one physical shelf of unsorted CDs that you want to process.

  1. Bring the CDs from that shelf to the newsroom.
  2. On Shelf Sorter, enter the shelf label. (e.g. 23F)
  3. Press enter to start a new source stack.
The shelf label identifies where this batch came from. It does not mean that the CDs will return to that shelf.

STEP 2

Scan the Entire Stack

Scan the CDs one at a time, beginning with the CD physically on top of the stack. Use the small barcode applied by the music librarians, not the barcode on the album's paper insert or case.

  1. Take the top CD from the source stack.
  2. Scan its barcode.
  3. Place the scanned CD onto a new stack.
  4. Take the next CD from the source stack.
  5. Repeat until every CD has been scanned.

This reversal is intentional. Do not flip the scanned stack afterward. Shelf Sorter expects the last CD you scanned to now be physically on top.

When the original stack is empty and every CD has been scanned, choose Finish Scanning.

STEP 3

Sort the Source Stack into Columns

Shelf Sorter will now walk through the physical stack from top to bottom and tell you which destination column each CD belongs in.

  1. Keep the scanned source stack exactly as it is.
  2. Take CDs from the top of the stack.
  3. Read the destination column shown on screen.
  4. Place each CD on top of that column's physical stack.
  5. Confirm the group on screen.
  6. Continue until the source stack is empty.
Several consecutive CDs may belong to the same column. The sorter groups these together so that you can move them efficiently. Ensure that you place each album one-by-one, instead of in a group, as Shelf Sorter expects that albums are placed into column stacks individually.

STEP 4

Keep the Column Stacks

Column stacks are persistent. You do not need to immediately sort a column into its final shelves.

More unsorted source shelves can be scanned and added to the existing column stacks. Each new CD should be placed on top of the appropriate physical column stack exactly as Shelf Sorter instructs.

Do not rearrange a column stack. Shelf Sorter remembers its exact physical order so that the stack can be processed later.

STEP 5

Sort a Column into Final Shelves

Once you are ready to finish a column, choose that column from the Shelf Sorter dashboard.

  1. Bring the corresponding physical column stack to the shelf sorting area.
  2. Do not flip or reorder it.
  3. Select the same column number in Shelf Sorter.
  4. Take CDs from the physical top of the column stack.
  5. Follow the destination shelf shown on screen.
  6. Place each CD onto its destination shelf stack.
  7. Confirm each group as you complete it.

When the session is complete, the column stack should be physically empty and Shelf Sorter should also show that the column has been cleared.

Unknown CDs

A barcode may occasionally scan successfully but not match an album in the catalog.

Shelf Sorter treats these CDs as a separate set-aside pile rather than guessing where they belong.

If Shelf Sorter tells you to set a CD aside, put it in the designated stack. Do not place it into a destination column - the music librarians will handle these albums later.

Mistakes & Undo

If you realize that you placed the most recent group incorrectly, use Undo before continuing.

Physically reverse whatever movement you just made at the same time. The website and the real stacks should always agree.

Do not use Undo without also undoing the physical move. Likewise, do not physically move CDs backward without telling Shelf Sorter.

Understanding Stack Order

Every time CDs are moved one at a time from the top of one stack onto the top of another stack, their order reverses. 

ORIGINAL SHELF
TOP → [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] → BOTTOM

        ↓ scan

SCANNED SOURCE STACK
TOP → [5, 4, 3, 2, 1] → BOTTOM

        ↓ sort into one column

COLUMN STACK
TOP → [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] → BOTTOM

        ↓ sort into one shelf

FINAL SHELF STACK
TOP → [5, 4, 3, 2, 1] → BOTTOM

Shelf Sorter handles these reversals automatically. Your job is simply to preserve the physical stack order and follow the instructions on screen.

Important Rules

✓ DO

Always work from the physical top of the stack.

✓ DO

Keep the website and physical stacks synchronized.

✓ DO

Use Undo immediately when a mistake happens.

✕ DON'T

Flip, shuffle, alphabetize, or otherwise reorder a working stack.

✕ DON'T

Remove CDs from the middle or bottom of a working stack.

✕ DON'T

Guess where an unknown CD belongs.

Troubleshooting

There's not enough room in the destination shelf

This may happen. Either through DJs not putting CDs back correctly, sorters not putting CDs in correct destination shelves - mistakes happen. Place as many albums as you can into the correct destination shelf, and then place the rest in the designated pile in the newsroom.

The CD on screen is not the CD on top of my stack.

Stop sorting. The physical stack and Shelf Sorter may be out of sync. Do not continue moving CDs until the discrepancy has been resolved.

I put a CD or group in the wrong place.

Use Undo and physically return the affected CDs to their previous stack in the corresponding order.

A barcode isn't recognized.

Try scanning it again. If Shelf Sorter accepts the barcode but identifies the CD as unknown, follow the set-aside instruction.

I accidentally changed the order of a stack.

Stop before processing anything else. Do not guess the original order. Ask the project manager for help restoring the stack.
